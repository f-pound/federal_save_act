What the applicant presents
Where does this list come from?

It is the statute's own list. The SAVE Act (new NVRA § 3(b)) defines "documentary proof of United States citizenship" as exactly these documents, in three groups: four that count on their own; an ordinary government photo ID that counts only when paired; and six supporting documents that count only with that photo ID.

We typed that definition once, as structured data (data/parsed/federal_save_act_document_rules.json — each entry carries the section number and the exact statutory wording). A small compiler turns that one file into three things that therefore cannot disagree: the ACL2 definitions the 219 theorems are proved against, a plain-English paraphrase, and the checkboxes you see here. A CI check also confirms every quoted clause appears verbatim in both the H.R. 22 text and the current S. 1383 vehicle.

So when the clerk says "accepted" or "denied", that is the statute's list applied mechanically — not an opinion.

What the voter carries
Where does this list come from?

New HAVA § 303A(c), added by § 3 of the SAVE America Act, defines "valid photo identification" as exactly five documents. Three of them must show a photo and an expiration date. It is a different list from the registration list above: a passport is on both; an ordinary photo ID paired with a birth certificate registers you but is not valid photo ID at the polls unless it carries an expiration date; a driver's licence gets you a ballot but does not prove citizenship at registration.

Without valid ID the voter may cast a provisional ballot, counted only if ID — or a religious-objection affidavit — is presented within 3 days (§ 303A(a)(1)(B)). These checkboxes, the ACL2 definitions and the English paraphrase are compiled from one file, and CI checks every quoted clause is verbatim in S. 1383.

What Is This?

This is a Computational Amicus Explorer — an interactive tool that visualizes how a formal mathematical proof system (ACL2) analyzes the constitutional implications of the Federal SAVE Act (H.R. 22).

What does ACL2 do here?

ACL2 is a theorem prover used in hardware and software verification. In this project, it models the SAVE Act's voter registration process as a state machine and derives conditional conclusions about constitutional conflicts — given a set of disclosed assumptions.

ACL2 does not decide the case. It proves conditional consequences from disclosed assumptions. Humans still decide empirical truth, doctrinal soundness, and which interpretation a court should adopt.

How to use it — three minutes

1
Try the two scenes at the top. Hand the clerk documents at the registration counter, then carry ID to the polling place. The clerk answers from the statute's own lists (compiled from one source file so they cannot drift from the proofs), the court's reading of the attestation process decides the hinge, and your premises decide what a denied citizen means.
2
Pick a point of view in the left panel — Both sides, Challenger's case, Government's defense, Statute text only, Citizenship implies documents, Contested premises only. Each is a complete, valid argument; the description tells you which factual claim it rests on.
3
Untick any premise you doubt. Boxes that depended on it dim; conclusion badges flip between Supported and Unsupported. Nothing here is a grey area of logic — every grey area is a choice, and each box says whose: legislature (statutory text), court (doctrine / interpretation), fact-finder (empirical), or party stipulation (conceded by both sides).
4
Click any box for its legal source, the ACL2 theorem or axiom name, the book it lives in, who decides it, and what it depends on. Theorems say "Nobody — proved by ACL2 from definitions."
5
Export a memo. The Export memo button writes your current premises, their deciders and sources, and the resulting conditional conclusions as a Markdown brief you can paste into a document.
6
For reviewers: the header numbers open drawers listing every certified book, theorem and traced axiom. Everything is machine-checked in CI on every commit — all books certified, every quoted clause verbatim in both bill texts, generated books identical to their source files.
